# Alice and Bot, version 1

Use [the cast sheet](alice-bot-v1.png) as the primary identity reference.
The [pilot](../style/pencil-pilot-v1.png) supplies the pencil texture, palette,
rough diagram conventions, and ash-tree setting. Character identity wins
where the references disagree.

| Character | Identity anchors | Flexible features |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Alice | Dark brown curly hair, round dark glasses, teal knitted sweater, warm light-medium skin, charcoal trousers, dark shoes | Pose, expression, questions and discoveries |
| Bot | Ochre rounded head and body, black face screen, pale eyes, one ball-tipped antenna, rounded segmented limbs, one central chest button | Eye expression, gestures and reactions |

Bot's single central chest button is the adopted design. The pilot had two
chest details. Do not reproduce that discrepancy in later episodes. The
reference sheet's labelled height target and its drawing are approximate;
create a measured silhouette guide before scenes depending on precise height.

Alice and Bot are named on-page in the example episode. Bob and Carol are
reserved names with no established appearance; introduce them by authoring
and reviewing their own references first. Character roles remain flexible:
anyone can make a prediction, find a counterexample or learn from a result.

The ash setting uses compound leaves with paired narrow leaflets. Branches
and drifting leaves frame the action without covering dialogue. Teal and
rough arrows mark a selected field or arm, but text and placement must carry
the distinction in grayscale too.

A deliberate redesign gets a new reference filename and catalogue ID. Do
not overwrite old references and then claim older images used the new design.
